# Go-To-Market (GTM) Strategy

## 1. The Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)
[cite_start]Our exact target user is not a vague category like "startups"[cite: 170]. [cite_start]We are targeting **Engineering Managers (EMs), Chief Technology Officers (CTOs), and solo Technical Founders** at **Pre-Seed to Series A startups (team sizes between 5 to 50 developers)**[cite: 170]. 

At this operational stage, the company has scaled past individual hobby tiers and is buying seat licenses rapidly, but they lack a dedicated procurement or finance department to audit SaaS waste.

## 2. User Intent Triggers
Right before a user needs this tool, they are typical experiencing or searching for:
* Searching for alternatives to clear seat limits (e.g., "Cursor business vs pro seats overlap").
* Reviewing monthly credit card statements and noticing unpredicted spikes in API billing metrics.
* Scrolling through tactical tech threads debating ecosystem consolidation (e.g., "Dropping Copilot for Claude Code").

## 3. Targeted Zero-Dollar Distribution Channels
[cite_start]To acquire our first 100 users in 30 days with a $0 paid budget, we will bypass generic marketing and use high-density niche communities:
* **Y-Combinator & Indie Hackers Networks:** Launching a direct, non-promotional post on Hacker News titled "Show HN: Mint for AI Tool Spend – See where your team is double-paying retail premiums."
* **Specific Subreddits:** Engaging actively inside `r/LocalLLaMA`, `r/SaaS`, and `r/webdev` by solving users' existing architecture threads and dropping the tool as a free tool to calculate their configurations.
* [cite_start]**Curated X (Twitter) Founder Lists:** Direct messaging tech founders on Twitter who run early-stage development teams, offering a 60-second structural cost check with zero authentication required[cite: 41, 190].
